01

How to structure a data analytics case study

Every strong case study follows the same six-step path. Each section should stay short — the goal is a reader who can follow the logic from start to finish without guessing what you did or why.

  1. Problem: the business situation and the decision to be made.
  2. Questions: two or three specific, answerable questions.
  3. Data: what data exists, its limits and how you cleaned it.
  4. Analysis: the method, with the main charts or queries.
  5. Findings: what the data shows, stated plainly.
  6. Recommendation: what the business should do, and what could go wrong.
02

Common case-study problem types

These five problem types appear repeatedly in interviews and portfolio projects. Each pairs a typical business question with the analysis that normally answers it.

Problem type Typical question Typical analysis
Sales decline Why did sales fall in one region? Trend by region, product and period; compare to the previous year
Customer churn Which customers are leaving and why? Segment by tenure, usage and complaints; compare churned and retained groups
Employee attrition Which groups leave most often? Attrition rate by department, tenure and manager
Marketing campaign Did the campaign work? Conversion by channel; cost per acquisition; test vs control
Inventory Which products are over- or under-stocked? Stock turnover and days of cover by product
03

What reviewers look for — and what to avoid

Reviewers look for

  • A clear question before any chart.
  • Honest handling of missing or odd data.
  • Numbers that reconcile with the source.
  • A recommendation that follows from the evidence, with limits stated.

Common mistakes

  • Starting with the tool, not the question.
  • Showing every chart you made — keep only the ones that support the answer.
  • Overclaiming causes: a pattern is not proof of why it happened.
  • No recommendation: analysis without a decision is incomplete.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a data analytics case study?
It is a worked example that takes one business problem through the data, the analysis and the findings to a recommendation. It shows how an analyst thinks.
How do I structure a data analytics case study?
Use problem, questions, data, analysis, findings and recommendation, in that order. Keep each section short and tie the conclusion to the evidence.
How is a case study different from a project?
A project builds something, such as a dashboard. A case study explains how a specific business problem was analysed and what was decided. Many portfolios include both.
Are case studies asked in data analyst interviews?
Often, in some form. Employers may give a small dataset and a question and ask you to explain your approach. Practising the structure above helps.
Which tools can I use for a case study?
Excel, SQL, Power BI, Tableau or Python are all suitable. Choose the tool that fits the data size and the question.
